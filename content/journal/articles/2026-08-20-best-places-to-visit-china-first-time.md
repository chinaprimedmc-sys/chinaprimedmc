## Draft

For most first-time visitors, **Beijing, Xi'an and Shanghai make the strongest three-city introduction to China**. Beijing carries the imperial story and the Great Wall. Xi'an gives that history an archaeological scale at the Terracotta Army. Shanghai brings the journey into the present and works well as an international departure point. With 10 to 12 days, that route is coherent, varied and possible without packing every other morning.

Chengdu, Guilin and Zhangjiajie are not lesser choices. Each is a better fourth destination for a different traveler. The mistake is adding all three because they appear on a list of China's best places.

| Destination         | The reason to go                             | Useful first-trip stay | What the itinerary must absorb                           |
| ------------------- | -------------------------------------------- | ---------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Beijing             | Great Wall, Forbidden City, imperial history | 4-5 nights             | Large sites, real walking, one full Wall day             |
| Xi'an               | Terracotta Army, Silk Road history, food     | 2-3 nights             | A substantial museum day and an intercity transfer       |
| Shanghai            | Architecture, design, food, modern China     | 2-3 nights             | Less ancient depth; day trips need extra time            |
| Chengdu             | Pandas, Sichuan food, relaxed street life    | 2-3 nights             | Another flight or rail sector and early panda morning    |
| Guilin and Yangshuo | Karst landscape and countryside              | 3 nights               | Weather sensitivity and road/river logistics             |
| Zhangjiajie         | Vertical sandstone landscapes                | 3-4 nights             | Long internal transfers, queues, stairs and weather risk |

## Beijing: the place we cut around, not the place we cut

Beijing is the clearest answer when a first trip must include the Great Wall and the Forbidden City. It also needs more time than its attraction list suggests.

The Forbidden City is not a two-hour photo stop once the approach through Tian'anmen, security, courtyards and exit logistics are counted. Mutianyu is a separate day outside the city. The Temple of Heaven is most alive in the morning, while the Summer Palace deserves an afternoon rather than whatever remains after another landmark.

In routes we operate, four nights is the practical minimum and five is often better. The extra night is not empty. It prevents the Great Wall from sharing a day with a second major site and gives weather or energy somewhere to move.

**Choose Beijing if:** China's dynastic history is central to why you are coming.

**Do not give Beijing two nights because:** an international arrival day and a Great Wall day can consume almost the entire stay before the city itself has begun.

## Xi'an: the city that makes the historical route complete

Xi'an is often described as a Terracotta Army stop. That undersells it. The city explains why an imperial capital sat at the eastern end of the Silk Road and why the army was built where it was.

Two nights allow one arrival evening and one proper Terracotta Army day. Three nights create room for the city wall, Muslim Quarter, Great Mosque area, food and a less transactional encounter with the city. We normally place the Terracotta Army on its own day because the complex involves distance, crowds and long periods on hard floors.

Beijing to Xi'an is one of the strongest high-speed rail sectors for a first itinerary: city-to-city, visually legible and easier to understand than an airport sequence. The rail journey still includes hotel checkout, station security, boarding and the next hotel transfer. It is a transfer day, not a blank line.

**Choose Xi'an if:** the Terracotta Army is non-negotiable or you want history to feel larger than palace architecture.

**Skip Xi'an if:** archaeology does not interest you and those nights would serve a landscape or food priority better.

## Shanghai: a strong ending, not merely the modern contrast

Shanghai works especially well at the end of a Beijing-to-Xi'an route. The skyline provides the obvious contrast, but the city's value is more intimate: the scale change between the Bund, former concession streets, lane houses, contemporary galleries and neighborhood food.

Two nights can show the central city. Three allow a more deliberate day or a water-town excursion. The airport matters: Pudong serves most long-haul flights, while Hongqiao is better connected to domestic air and high-speed rail. A hotel that looks central on a map can still create poor airport or sightseeing transfers at the wrong time of day.

**Choose Shanghai if:** design, food, architecture and contemporary urban life matter as much as monuments.

**Start or finish there if:** an open-jaw international ticket avoids flying back across China only to go home.

## Chengdu: the best fourth city for food and pandas

Chengdu changes the emotional register of a first trip. The panda base supplies the headline, but the city earns its nights through tea houses, markets, Sichuan food and a social rhythm unlike Beijing or Shanghai.

The panda visit should be planned early in the day, with the base chosen only after checking current exhibits and access. A rushed itinerary that flies in, sees pandas and flies out uses two transfers for one attraction. We prefer at least two nights; three if food is a genuine interest or Leshan belongs in the route.

**Add Chengdu if:** pandas and food are among the top three reasons for the trip.

**Do not add it if:** the only available slot is a one-night detour between already short stays.

## Guilin and Yangshuo: choose them for landscape, not for a checklist

The Li River and karst country give a first journey a visual language that cities cannot. They also depend more on weather, water conditions and the exact hotel location.

Guilin is the transport gateway; Yangshuo is usually where travelers imagine staying. Moving between them, joining a river experience and reaching rural areas takes time. Three nights is a useful starting point. Two can work with favorable flights and a narrow plan, but leaves little resilience.

**Add Guilin and Yangshuo if:** landscape is more important than adding another famous city.

**Choose the hotel carefully because:** a beautiful rural property can be exactly right for stillness and exactly wrong for repeated town visits.

## Zhangjiajie: spectacular, but expensive in itinerary time

Zhangjiajie is not one mountain beside one airport. The national forest park, Tianmen Mountain, Grand Canyon area and common hotel bases sit in different operating patterns. Lifts reduce some climbing, but they do not remove standing, queues, uneven paths or the need to move between transport systems.

Three nights is the minimum we would use for a meaningful visit; four gives weather more room. For a 10-day first trip, Zhangjiajie usually replaces Xi'an or Shanghai rather than joining both.

**Add Zhangjiajie if:** natural scenery is the reason you chose China over another destination.

**Leave it for a second trip if:** you mainly want the Great Wall, imperial history and an efficient first overview.

## The route test we use before adding a city

Every proposed destination must answer four questions:

1. What experience does it add that the route does not already contain?
2. How many hotel nights does that experience need to work properly?
3. What is the full hotel-to-hotel transfer, not just the train or flight time?
4. Which existing city or free period leaves to make room?

The fourth answer is where most itineraries become honest. A new city is not added to empty space. It is purchased with time that currently belongs to somewhere else.

## Three first-trip routes that make sense

### 10 days: Beijing, Xi'an and Shanghai

Use four nights in Beijing, two in Xi'an and three in Shanghai, with the remaining day accounted for by arrival and the two intercity movements. This is the classic route at its firmest workable pace.

For a commercial reference, our published [nine-day private version](/tours/first-china-beautifully-paced) starts from **US$3,190 per person**, based on four guests sharing two rooms outside peak periods. A ten-day version needs the additional night and whichever service is attached to it quoted explicitly.

### 12 days: Beijing, Xi'an and Shanghai with room to choose

Five nights in Beijing, three in Xi'an and three in Shanghai allow the major sights to stand alone and create at least one day that can respond to weather, energy or a personal interest.

### 14 days: Beijing, Xi'an, Chengdu and Shanghai

Four cities work when the fourth has a clear job. Chengdu adds pandas, food and a different regional culture. The route needs disciplined transport choices and should not hide sightseeing inside both transfer days.

Our published [11-day four-city journey](/tours/beijing-xian-chengdu-shanghai-private-11-day-tour) starts from **US$4,290 per person** on the same four-guest, two-room, outside-peak basis. It proves that we operate the geography and publish a baseline; it is not a 14-day quote. The extra nights, private-service days and room choices must be costed rather than inferred.

## FAQ

### What are the best places to visit in China for a first trip?

Beijing, Xi'an and Shanghai provide the strongest general introduction for most first-time visitors. Add Chengdu for pandas and food, Guilin for river landscapes or Zhangjiajie for dramatic mountains only when that experience justifies the extra transfer.

### How many cities should I visit in China in two weeks?

Three cities create a more spacious journey. Four can work when transport is efficient and each destination has a distinct purpose. Five usually turns too much of a first trip into packing, stations, airports and hotel check-in.

### Is Beijing or Shanghai better for first-time visitors?

Beijing is stronger for the Great Wall and imperial history. Shanghai is easier for contemporary culture, food and an international gateway. Many first routes use both, entering through one and leaving through the other.

### Should I visit Guilin or Zhangjiajie?

Choose Guilin and Yangshuo for river scenery, countryside and a gentler landscape stay. Choose Zhangjiajie for more dramatic mountain infrastructure and accept greater walking, queue and weather complexity.
