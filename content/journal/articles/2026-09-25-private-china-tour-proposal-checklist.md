## Draft

A strong private China tour proposal should let you answer five questions without guessing: **Who is responsible? What exactly is being purchased? Which parts are confirmed? What could change? What happens if they do?** If the document is rich in adjectives but vague on those points, it is not ready for a deposit.

Use this checklist to compare proposals on the same basis. The cheapest and most expensive options can both hide gaps. The goal is not to demand that every attraction ticket be issued months in advance. It is to know which party owns each task, which assumptions support the price, and how the trip will be operated in China.

| Proposal area         | A decision-ready proposal shows                                       | A warning sign                                          |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Contracting party     | Legal entity, contact details and applicable terms                    | Only a brand or social-media account is named           |
| Price                 | Currency, traveler count, room basis and price unit                   | A total without the assumptions behind it               |
| Hotels                | Property or stated standard, room type, meals and confirmation status | “Luxury hotel” with no location or room basis           |
| Guiding and transport | Cities, service hours, vehicle scope and handoffs                     | “Private service” without responsibility or limits      |
| Tickets and trains    | Planned or confirmed status, document needs and fallback              | Every item appears confirmed before sale windows open   |
| Changes               | Cancellation terms, substitution standard and contact process         | “Subject to availability” with no remedy or alternative |

## 1. Identify the company that will take your money and operate the trip

Start with the contracting entity, not the website design. Ask for the company's legal name, business address, contact details, payment recipient and booking terms. If an overseas advisor, marketplace or independent designer is involved, understand who is the retailer, who is the China operator and who is responsible when a local service fails.

AVIORA publishes its operating identity because trust should survive outside a sales conversation. The proposal and payment instructions should use names that can be reconciled with that identity. A different beneficiary is not automatically improper, but it requires a clear explanation before money moves.

Questions to ask:

- Which legal entity is accepting the booking?
- Which entity will appear on the invoice or receipt?
- Who contracts the guides, vehicles, hotels and domestic transport?
- Who can make an operational decision while I am in China?
- Which written terms govern the purchase?

## 2. Make the price unit impossible to misunderstand

“From $3,000” is not enough. The proposal should say whether the amount is **per person, per room, per vehicle or per private party**, and what traveler count produced it. Private trip pricing changes with room occupancy, vehicle size, guide allocation, domestic transport and whether costs are shared among two, four or six travelers.

Confirm:

- Currency and total price
- Number and ages of travelers
- Number of rooms and occupancy in each room
- Single supplements or child pricing assumptions
- Domestic flight or rail class
- Included service charges and known taxes
- Card, transfer or currency-conversion fees
- Deposit, balance date and refund method

If “7+ travelers” is shown, the proposal should not silently calculate seven exact travelers. The actual party size can change the vehicle, luggage plan, room count and guide arrangement.

## 3. Read the route as an operating plan, not a list of attractions

An itinerary can include all the right names and still be difficult to operate. Read every day for sequence, movement and recovery. Arrival, departure and hotel-change days should not be described as if they provide the same usable time as a full day in one city.

For each day, look for:

- Starting city and ending city
- Principal experience and realistic supporting stops
- Intercity transport or long road sector
- Hotel change or luggage handoff
- Meals actually included
- Walking, stairs, standing or altitude that may matter
- What the guide and vehicle cover
- What can be adapted if weather, closure or traveler energy changes

The proposal does not need minute-by-minute timing months ahead. It does need enough structure to show that the day can work.

## 4. Make hotel language specific enough to protect the stay

Hotel quality is not captured by star count alone. Location, room category, bedding, breakfast, occupancy, accessibility and the relationship between rooms can matter more than the lobby.

Ask the proposal to state:

- Hotel name, or the exact standard if a property is not yet selected
- Neighborhood and why it suits the itinerary
- Room category and bedding request
- Breakfast inclusion
- Number of guests permitted in the room
- Connecting, adjoining or nearby-room request status
- Cancellation or substitution basis
- Whether the hotel is confirmed, on request or illustrative

If a named hotel becomes unavailable, “similar” should be judged by the function it served: location, room setup, service level and journey flow, not only a nominal star category.

## 5. Define what “private guide and driver” means

Private service varies widely. A proposal should identify where the guide meets you, whether the driver and guide are separate people, how long the service lasts, and whether airport, station, evening or free-day support is included.

Useful details include:

- Guide language and destination coverage
- Vehicle class or capacity appropriate to passengers and luggage
- Service hours and overtime basis
- Airport and station meeting method
- Whether the vehicle remains available between stops
- Who handles rail-station entry and platform orientation
- Child-seat requests and whether they are confirmed
- Who to contact if the guide or vehicle is delayed

Premium service should reduce uncertainty, not merely add a polished vehicle description.

## 6. Separate planned reservations from confirmed reservations

China Railway uses real-name ticketing, and foreign passengers use valid passport details for purchase and travel. High-demand attractions may also require timed, passport-linked reservations. Those facts make document accuracy and status language important.

Every reservation-dependent item should be labeled as one of the following:

1. Confirmed and issued
2. Checked and on request
3. Planned for purchase when the official sales window opens
4. Illustrative and not included

Ask what happens if the preferred time, train or attraction ticket is unavailable. A useful fallback protects the purpose of the day. It should not simply replace a major experience with free time and leave the value unchanged.

## 7. Check every inclusion and exclusion that changes the lived trip

Long inclusion lists can still avoid the important questions. Focus on services that create cost or friction on the ground.

### Accommodation

- Number of hotel nights
- Room and breakfast basis
- Early check-in or late checkout, if promised

### Movement

- Airport and station transfers
- Intercity trains or flights and class
- Checked-baggage allowance for domestic air
- Private vehicle days and service limits

### Touring

- Guide days and language
- Admissions and cable cars or shuttle buses
- Experiences that require separate booking

### Meals

- Exactly which breakfasts, lunches and dinners are included
- Whether a welcome dinner has a budget or set menu
- How allergies and dietary needs will be communicated

### Support

- Pre-trip contact
- On-trip emergency or operations contact
- Response channel and relevant hours

Do not assume that a meal, transfer or ticket is included because it appears in the day description. The commercial inclusion list should agree with the itinerary.

## 8. Understand the payment and cancellation logic

Read the schedule as a risk allocation. A deposit may allow the operator to secure hotels or services. The proposal should say when the balance is due, which payments are refundable, what supplier penalties may apply and how changes are calculated.

Ask:

- Is the deposit a percentage or fixed amount?
- Which services will be booked after payment?
- When does each cancellation tier begin?
- Are supplier penalties passed through at cost?
- How are traveler-requested changes priced?
- What happens if AVIORA or a supplier must substitute a service?
- In which currency will a refund be made?
- Is travel insurance recommended, and what does the operator not cover?

No tour company can promise that every disruption will disappear. A trustworthy proposal explains the process and responsibility before a disruption occurs.

## 9. Test the proposal against your actual party

A proposal can be operationally sound and still be wrong for the travelers. Before accepting it, check the route against the oldest and youngest traveler, the person with the most restrictive diet, the lightest sleeper, the slowest comfortable walker and anyone who needs a particular room setup.

This is not about designing the whole trip around one limitation. It is about finding the days where a hidden mismatch could affect everyone.

Useful disclosures include:

- Comfortable continuous walking time
- Stairs, uneven surfaces or standing concerns
- Dietary requirements and cross-contact sensitivity
- Medication or rest routines relevant to the day
- Child ages and car-seat needs
- Luggage volume
- Celebration timing
- Preferred balance of guided and independent time

## 10. Look for evidence of a real handoff system

The proposal should show how planning becomes travel. Ask what you receive after booking and before arrival. A strong process usually includes a final itinerary, hotel and transport details, passport data check, arrival meeting point, emergency contact and a method for communicating changes.

The question is not whether there is an app. It is whether the right person has the right information at each handoff.

> A premium proposal is not the one with the most pages. It is the one that leaves the fewest important responsibilities implied.

## A copy-and-use checklist

- I know the legal contracting entity and payment recipient.
- The price unit, currency, traveler count and room assumptions are explicit.
- Arrival, departure and transfer days are described honestly.
- Hotels or hotel standards include location, room and meal details.
- Guide, driver and vehicle coverage are defined by city and day.
- Trains, flights and admissions show their confirmation status.
- Inclusions and exclusions agree with the day-by-day itinerary.
- Payment, cancellation and change terms are attached.
- The proposal addresses our party's walking, room, diet and luggage needs.
- I know who supports us before and during the trip.
- Material alternatives are explained before the deposit.
- The final written confirmation will replace assumptions with booked details.

## FAQ

### Should a private China tour proposal name every hotel before payment?

It should name the hotel or define the promised standard, location and room basis clearly enough to price and compare the trip. If a property is illustrative or on request, that status and the substitution rule should be explicit.

### Is “subject to availability” normal?

Yes, for inventory that cannot yet be confirmed. It is not enough by itself. The proposal should identify which item is subject to availability, when it will be checked and what comparable alternative or refund logic applies.

### How can I verify a China tour operator?

Ask for the legal company name, tourism-business status, business address, written terms and payment beneficiary. Confirm how any overseas seller or advisor relates to the China operating entity. Keep copies of the proposal, invoice, terms and payment record.

### Should attraction tickets be confirmed months ahead?

Not always. Official sales windows differ and can change. The proposal should distinguish a planned attraction from an issued reservation and state who will secure it when booking opens.

### What is the most important question to ask before paying?

Ask: “Which parts of this proposal are confirmed today, which are assumptions, and what will happen if an assumed item cannot be secured?” The answer reveals both transparency and operational thinking.
