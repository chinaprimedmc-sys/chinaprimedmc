# China’s 240-Hour Transit Rule

> [!summary] Editorial brief
> **Audience:** Eligible international travelers considering a short China stopover between two countries or regions  
> **Search intent:** Check route eligibility before booking  
> **Primary keyword:** China 240-hour visa-free transit  
> **Business relevance:** Converts a complex entry question into a viable Beijing, Shanghai or multi-city private stopover brief

## Draft

China’s 240-hour visa-free transit policy can allow an eligible traveler to enter through an approved port, remain within the permitted area for up to 240 hours, and depart for a third country or region without obtaining a visa in advance. It is not a general ten-day visa waiver. Nationality, travel-document validity, arrival port, onward routing, confirmed departure and geographic limits must all work together.

The safest way to assess a trip is route first, not nationality first. Write the journey as **origin → mainland China → onward country or region**, then check every element against the current National Immigration Administration rules before buying non-refundable travel.

## The policy at a glance

The National Immigration Administration’s November 2025 announcement lists **55 eligible countries**, **65 eligible entry ports** and permitted areas across **24 provincial-level regions**. An applicant must hold a qualifying international travel document valid for at least three months and a confirmed onward ticket to a third country or region within 240 hours. Immigration authorities make the entry decision at the port.

The stay is calculated from 00:00 on the day after entry. That calculation may create more clock time than simply counting ten 24-hour blocks from landing, but it should never be used to plan a departure beyond the authorized period shown by immigration.

### The essential route test

A straightforward qualifying pattern looks like this:

**London → Shanghai → Tokyo**

The traveler arrives from the United Kingdom and continues to Japan. By contrast, **London → Shanghai → London** is not transit to a third country or region. A connection elsewhere may affect the analysis, so use the complete ticketed sequence rather than only the long-haul flight numbers.

Hong Kong SAR and Macao SAR are treated as regions for international transit routing, subject to the full policy and the traveler’s ability to enter the onward destination. A route such as **Singapore → Beijing → Hong Kong** may therefore satisfy the third-region structure, but nationality, port and permitted-stay conditions still require confirmation.

## Five checks before booking

### 1. Confirm the traveler’s exact nationality

Eligibility follows the passport used for entry. Do not rely on country lists copied into old articles: the official list changed when Indonesia was added and the eligible-port network expanded in November 2025. Dual nationals should assess the journey using the document they will present throughout the booking and border process.

### 2. Confirm the passport and onward-entry documents

The NIA conditions require a valid international travel document with at least three months’ validity and eligibility to enter the onward country or region. Airlines may apply their own document checks before boarding. Carry the itinerary, accommodation confirmations and onward ticket in an accessible offline format.

### 3. Use an eligible entry port

Not every international gateway participates. The current list includes major airports such as Beijing Capital, Beijing Daxing, Shanghai Pudong, Shanghai Hongqiao, Chengdu Tianfu, Xi’an Xianyang, Zhangjiajie Hehua and others, as well as selected sea, rail and land ports. Match the exact port—not merely the city—to the official appendix.

### 4. Keep the itinerary inside the permitted area

The policy does not automatically open all of mainland China. Permitted movement depends on the entry location and the areas listed by the NIA. Beijing entry can support planning within the approved Beijing–Tianjin–Hebei area; Shanghai entry sits within a broader approved Yangtze River Delta area. Never add a domestic side trip until it has been checked against the permitted-stay table.

### 5. Protect the onward departure

The onward booking needs a specified date and confirmed arrangement within the allowed window. Separate tickets may be workable, but they deserve extra scrutiny because the arriving carrier and border officers need to see a credible, compliant departure. Leave operational buffer for summer storms, winter disruption or a long surface transfer.

## Choosing a stopover that fits the rule

A good transit itinerary is deliberately smaller than a normal China holiday. For Beijing, three to five well-paced nights can cover the Forbidden City, Temple of Heaven, a hutong context day and a separate Great Wall day without treating the capital as an airport layover. Review AVIORA’s [Beijing destination guide](/destinations/beijing) and [five-day Beijing route](/tours/beijing-great-wall-private-5-day-tour) as planning references, then confirm that the chosen entry and exit ports remain compliant.

Shanghai can work as a softer urban stop: the Bund, selected neighborhoods, museums and one carefully chosen regional extension if permitted. The [Shanghai destination guide](/destinations/shanghai) explains how the city can function as either an introduction or a finale.

Do not force Beijing, Xi’an and Shanghai into one transit window simply because the trains are fast. The policy clock, permitted geography and connection risk matter more than the number of map points.

## What to present on arrival

Keep the following together:

- the passport used for the journey;
- the confirmed onward ticket and any relevant visa or entry approval for that destination;
- accommodation details for each night;
- a clear itinerary inside the permitted area;
- contact details for the host, hotel or local operator; and
- the completed arrival information required at the time of travel.

Since November 2025, foreign arrivals can complete arrival-card information online through official NIA channels, with port options remaining for travelers who cannot do so in advance. This is a process improvement, not advance approval of visa-free transit.

## When a regular visa may be better

Apply for the appropriate visa instead when the route returns to the same country or region, includes locations outside the permitted area, lacks a firm onward ticket, exceeds the time limit, or involves work, study or journalism. A regular visa may also be the calmer choice when weather-sensitive separate tickets leave little recovery time.

## FAQ

### Is 240-hour visa-free transit the same as visa-free entry?

No. Transit requires an eligible third-country or third-region routing and compliance with specified ports and permitted areas. A unilateral or mutual visa exemption has different eligibility and route conditions.

### Can I fly into Shanghai and leave from another Chinese city?

Only if both the ports and all travel between them are allowed under the current permitted-area rules. Check the NIA appendix for the exact route before booking.

### Does the 240 hours begin when my flight lands?

The NIA states that the visa-free stay period is calculated from 00:00 on the day following entry. Follow the period authorized by immigration and keep the departure comfortably inside it.

### Can AVIORA guarantee entry under the policy?

No travel company can guarantee a border decision. AVIORA can review itinerary logic and arrange services after the route is assessed, while travelers remain responsible for current eligibility and documents.
