# Listening Before Designing

## Draft

On July 30, 2026, TTG Asia published its report, [MATTA Connect gains traction as B2B platform](https://www.ttgasia.com/2026/07/30/matta-connect-gains-traction-as-b2b-platform/). Among the buyers, sellers and travel leaders featured in the story was Guohui Shi, inbound travel specialist at China Prime DMC, the China operating team behind AVIORA.

The mention is meaningful to us because it independently documents our team's participation. But the more useful story is not simply that our operating name appeared in the travel trade press. It is why AVIORA went to MATTA Connect in the first place.

We went to listen before designing.

## A meeting room before an itinerary

MATTA Connect brought the regional travel trade together in Ipoh, Malaysia. TTG Asia reported that the second edition attracted 300 local and international buyers and 100 sellers, creating a setting for direct conversations rather than broad consumer promotion.

For AVIORA, those conversations were a practical part of product design. Guohui attended to strengthen relationships with Malaysian outbound travel agents and to understand Malaysian travelers more clearly: what makes China feel exciting, what makes it feel difficult, and what details determine whether a private journey feels genuinely comfortable.

## China is familiar and unfamiliar at the same time

Many international travelers recognize the images of China before they understand its geography. They know the Great Wall, giant pandas, Shanghai's skyline, the Terracotta Warriors, Sichuan food and mountain landscapes. They may not yet know which cities connect naturally, how much time a route needs, or how different the travel experience feels from one region to another.

That gap matters. A route can look impressive on paper and still feel tiring, confusing or poorly matched to the people taking it.

Our work begins by translating China into choices that travelers can understand. Instead of asking a first-time visitor to choose from a long list of unfamiliar place names, we listen for what draws them here: history, food, family time, natural scenery, photography, modern cities or a slower cultural experience.

Only then should the destination sequence begin.

## What we heard in Malaysia

The Malaysia-China mutual visa exemption arrangement has reduced one layer of friction between the two countries. TTG Asia reported Guohui's observation that Malaysian demand for travel to China had grown steadily following its implementation.

Growing demand does not mean every traveler wants the same China trip. Conversations around the event reinforced the importance of details that standard itineraries often treat as secondary.

• Families need realistic days, not continuous sightseeing from morning to night.

• Travelers with older parents need honest walking expectations, rest points and fewer unnecessary hotel changes.

• Muslim travelers need halal dining and prayer timing discussed before arrival, not improvised after the journey begins.

• Private groups need clear room arrangements, vehicle capacity and luggage planning.

• First-time visitors need simple explanations of why each city belongs in the route.

![A discussion about Muslim-friendly private China travel in Malaysia](/home/editorial/muslim-travel-buyers-kuala-lumpur.webp)

_Discussing Muslim-friendly planning, family comfort and local support with travel buyers._

These are not decorative service promises. They influence the route, hotel location, transport sequence, guide brief, restaurant checks and the amount of unplanned space protected inside each day.

## Better journeys begin with better questions

When we design a private China journey, the first questions should not be how many attractions can fit into the schedule. They should be who is traveling, what they care about, what may make them uncomfortable and what kind of daily rhythm they enjoy.

For one family, an early panda visit and a long Sichuan lunch may matter more than adding another museum. For another, the correct Great Wall section and cable-car plan may determine whether three generations enjoy the day together. A Muslim couple may care as much about restaurant verification and prayer timing as the headline landmarks. A photographer may prefer fewer stops with better light.

Listening turns those needs into operating decisions.

That is the value of meeting overseas travel professionals face to face. They hear the questions, hesitations and expectations of their clients before those travelers ever arrive in China. When that knowledge reaches the local operating team, the product becomes more relevant and the journey becomes easier to trust.

## One AVIORA journey, operated in China

AVIORA is our brand and the name private travelers see throughout planning and travel. China Prime DMC is the China-based travel services identity used by the local team coordinating routes, private transport, guides, tickets, hotels and on-trip support.

For the traveler, this is one connected service: a conversation with AVIORA from the beginning, backed by people on the ground who are responsible for making the journey work in China.

That is why MATTA Connect mattered to us. It was not a stage for claiming that we already knew every market. It was an opportunity to ask better questions, hear different priorities and bring those lessons back into the way we design China travel.
