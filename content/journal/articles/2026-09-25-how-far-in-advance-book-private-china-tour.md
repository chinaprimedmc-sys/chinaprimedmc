## Draft

For most private China trips, begin serious planning **four to eight months before departure**. Start earlier for Golden Week, Chinese New Year, spring and fall peak dates, distinctive hotels, large families or a route with several domestic sectors. A well-structured trip can sometimes be arranged inside six to ten weeks, but the closer the departure date, the more the plan should be built around what can still be confirmed rather than what looked ideal in the first draft.

The useful question is not simply, “How early should I book?” It is, **“Which decisions should become firm at each stage?”** International flights, city order, room configuration, passport-linked reservations and local services do not all need to be purchased on the same day. Booking everything early can create avoidable change fees. Waiting on everything can remove the choices that made the route worth taking.

| Time before departure | Best use of the window                                                        | What should still be treated as provisional                          |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 8–12 months           | Travel window, traveler needs, route feasibility, major holiday risk          | Exact daily timings and most individual tickets                      |
| 4–8 months            | Gateways, city sequence, hotel standard, room setup, private service scope    | Date-sensitive admissions and rail services not yet on sale          |
| 2–4 months            | Hotels, guides, vehicles, domestic air where appropriate, operational review  | Final rail and attraction confirmation if sales windows are not open |
| Final 6–8 weeks       | Passport-linked tickets, train sectors, names, dietary notes, arrival handoff | Only genuine availability-dependent alternatives                     |
| Final 7–14 days       | Reconfirmations, meeting points, weather response, emergency contacts         | Nothing material should remain unexplained                           |

## The practical answer by travel situation

### Start eight to twelve months ahead when the trip has hard constraints

An early start is valuable when the journey must fit school holidays, a fixed cruise or conference, a multigenerational room arrangement, accessible-room requirements, a major public holiday or a specific hotel category. The extra time is not mainly for writing a longer itinerary. It is for testing the route before expensive pieces narrow it.

At this stage, a responsible planner should be able to answer:

- Which international gateways reduce backtracking?
- How many nights are genuinely available after arrival and departure are counted?
- Which cities earn their hotel change?
- Where will the trip be physically demanding?
- Which room types or family configurations may be scarce?
- Which elements are recommendations, and which are already confirmed?

This is also the right stage to identify dates that overlap with periods of unusually heavy domestic travel. A private vehicle and guide can improve the day, but they cannot create hotel inventory, empty a railway station or reverse an attraction's capacity controls.

### Four to eight months is the planning center for many private journeys

This window usually provides enough time to compare routes without forcing every detail into a premature booking. For a first journey through Beijing, Xi'an and Shanghai, the key work is to settle the city sequence, arrival and departure gateways, number of nights, hotel locations and the amount of support expected at each transfer.

The itinerary should now be specific enough to expose pressure. “Beijing sightseeing” is not specific enough. “Forbidden City morning, lunch near the next stop, then a quieter afternoon with a realistic vehicle pickup” is closer to an operable day. The difference matters because timed admission, walking, traffic, closure days and pickup restrictions interact.

> A route should become firm before its individual bookings do. Otherwise, each purchase starts dictating the trip.

### Two to four months can still produce an excellent trip

At two to four months, the process becomes more decisive. The planner should present a primary plan and clearly named alternatives, not keep reopening the whole country. Hotel and flight availability should be checked against the actual party, including room count, child ages and bedding needs.

This is a good moment to confirm private guides and vehicles in the places where they add the most value. “Private” should mean more than the absence of strangers. It should clarify who coordinates the day, who handles the vehicle, what hours are included, how airport and station handoffs work, and who responds if a booked service changes.

### Inside six to ten weeks, flexibility becomes part of quality

A short planning window does not automatically mean a poor trip. It does mean the operator should be candid about what is still available. A strong late plan may use a different hotel area, reverse the route, replace one sold-out train or remove an unnecessary city. Those can be intelligent changes if they protect the journey's purpose.

Be cautious when a late proposal looks identical to an early one and labels everything “subject to availability.” That phrase is sometimes unavoidable, but it should not substitute for checking. Ask which services have been checked, which are on request and what the fallback will be.

## Why China bookings cannot all be treated the same

### International flights define the outer frame

Flights affect more than price. They determine whether the first and last days are usable, whether a route moves forward, and whether an early arrival or late departure creates an uncomfortable hotel gap. Before buying a nonrefundable ticket, test the complete pattern: arrival airport, arrival time, departure airport, connection risk and the night count on the ground.

AVIORA's planning judgment is to avoid using a good airfare as the only reason for a poor gateway. A modest saving can disappear through an extra domestic flight, a backtrack or a hotel night that contributes little to the trip.

### Hotels should be selected by the days they support

The most expensive hotel is not automatically the best base. The right hotel should fit the party, room requirements, first or last transfer, and the actual geography of the sightseeing plan. Confirm the room category, bedding, breakfast, occupancy and cancellation terms in writing.

For international travelers, hotels handle accommodation registration using the guest's valid travel document. If the trip uses a private residence or another non-hotel stay, different registration responsibilities apply. This is one reason the accommodation plan should be known, not improvised after arrival.

### Rail is passport-linked and sale-window dependent

China Railway uses real-name ticketing. Foreign passengers purchase with valid passport details and should travel with the same document used for the booking. The official 12306 English service also states that tickets are subject to availability and that passengers should allow time for security, identity checks and station movement.

That creates two separate planning moments: decide that rail is right for the route early, then issue the specific train when the relevant service becomes available for sale. A proposal should not present an unissued train as confirmed.

### Major attractions require accurate passenger details

Many high-demand attractions use timed or passport-linked admission. The operating team should collect names and passport numbers securely, explain when they are needed, and distinguish a planned visit from a confirmed reservation. Exact release windows and procedures can change, so date-specific confirmation matters more than a generic promise made months earlier.

## A five-stage booking process that protects the trip

### 1. Establish the decision brief

Begin with dates, travelers, gateways, interests, comfortable pace, room needs and budget range. If one of these is unknown, label it unknown. False precision is less useful than an honest assumption.

### 2. Approve the route logic

Approve why each city belongs, how the route moves and where the hardest days sit. At this point, the traveler should understand the trade-offs: what is gained by adding Chengdu, what is lost by compressing Xi'an, or why a train may be preferable to a flight on one sector but not another.

### 3. Approve a written service scope

The scope should state hotels or hotel standards, room basis, guide and vehicle coverage, intercity transport, admissions, meals, airport or station transfers, support, exclusions and payment terms. “All arrangements included” is not a scope.

### 4. Confirm date-sensitive inventory

The operator should report which hotels, rooms and services are confirmed, which are on request and which will be purchased later because the official sales window is not open. Alternatives should be equivalent in function, not merely similar in price.

### 5. Complete the operational handoff

Before departure, receive emergency contacts, meeting instructions, final hotel details, domestic transport details, required documents and a clear first-day plan. The final itinerary should reflect what was actually booked, not the earliest marketing draft.

## What should be confirmed before paying a deposit

- The legal contracting entity and how it relates to the brand selling the trip
- The route, dates, traveler count and room assumptions used for the price
- The currency, payment method, schedule and bank or card fees
- The cancellation and change terms
- What is included and excluded
- Which named services are confirmed, on request or illustrative
- What happens if the quoted hotel or transport cannot be secured
- Who provides support before and during the trip

The deposit should buy a defined planning and booking commitment. It should not require the traveler to infer what the operator has accepted responsibility for.

## When waiting is the smarter decision

Do not rush into a deposit when the travelers disagree on the trip length, the international flights produce a different night count than the itinerary, passport validity needs attention, or a mobility or medical concern has not been discussed. Resolve the condition that changes the route first.

Waiting is also sensible when a company will not provide written terms, will not identify the contracting entity, or cannot explain the difference between a sample itinerary and a confirmed booking. Earlier is only better when the underlying plan is ready.

## FAQ

### Is one year too early to plan a private China trip?

No. One year is useful for fixed school holidays, major public holidays, multigenerational room needs, special celebrations or a complex route. Use the early months to settle the route and service standard; do not assume every individual ticket can or should be issued that early.

### Can a private China trip be booked one month before departure?

Sometimes. The operator should build around live availability, present realistic alternatives and identify every unconfirmed item. A shorter route with fewer hotel changes is often more reliable than forcing an ambitious original wish list into the remaining inventory.

### Should I buy international flights before contacting a tour company?

You can, but first test the complete flight pattern against the route. Arrival and departure cities, usable nights, connection risk and hotel timing can materially change the best itinerary.

### When are China train tickets confirmed?

Specific trains are confirmed only when the service is available for sale and the ticket has been issued. The route can be designed around rail earlier, but a proposal should distinguish the intended train from an issued ticket.

### What information will a China tour company need from me?

Expect to provide traveler names, passport details when reservations require them, dates, room needs, arrival and departure information, dietary requirements and any pace or mobility considerations relevant to the itinerary. Ask how sensitive data is collected and stored.
