## Draft

The best private China trips do not begin with a long list of attractions. They begin with a clear conversation about the travelers: what they want to understand, how quickly they like to move, what they need from hotels and transport, and where local support will make the experience easier.

The practical sequence is straightforward: define the travelers and priorities, choose a route that can work in real time, test the hotel and transfer logic, state what is included, and confirm what still depends on dates or inventory. AVIORA follows this process for tailor-made private journeys from its China-based team.

## 1. Start with the travelers, not the template

Before choosing cities, write down the information that changes the design:

- Travel dates or the date range
- Number of travelers and children’s ages
- Arrival and departure gateways
- Interests such as history, food, nature, architecture or everyday life
- Walking comfort, mobility considerations and recovery time
- Room needs, dietary requirements and luggage pattern
- Whether you prefer full guiding, independent time or a mixture

This is not administrative detail. A family with a stroller, a couple interested in food and a group with an early international departure need different days even if they visit the same cities.

## 2. Choose a route that respects travel time

China is large. A route can look impressive on a map and still feel exhausting when station transfers, security, hotel check-in and luggage are counted. A useful proposal separates full sightseeing days from arrival, departure and intercity movement days.

For a first visit, Beijing, Xi'an and Shanghai make a coherent introduction: imperial history, the Terracotta Army and a modern commercial city. The [first-China private journey](/tours/first-china-beautifully-paced) uses that logic while leaving room to adjust pace and support. Travelers who want more recovery can compare [China, Considered](/tours/china-at-an-easier-pace-12-day-private-tour). Travelers who want Chengdu can review the [four-city route](/tours/beijing-xian-chengdu-shanghai-private-11-day-tour), understanding that one more city also means another movement day.

## 3. Design the rhythm before adding special experiences

Premium planning is often about what is removed. A major heritage site deserves enough time for orientation, interpretation and a pause. A long rail day should not be sold as a normal sightseeing day. A free day should have a purpose, whether that is independent exploration, a local cultural option or recovery.

Ask of every proposed day:

1. What is the main experience?
2. How does the traveler get there and back?
3. What walking, stairs, waiting or standing is involved?
4. Where could the day become tiring or weather-sensitive?
5. What can be shortened without losing the reason for visiting?

The answer should be visible in the itinerary, not left for the guide to improvise after arrival.

## 4. Make hotels part of the route

Hotel choice is more than a star rating. Location affects the first and last movement of the day. Room category affects sleep and privacy. Breakfast timing affects an early departure. A family may need two nearby rooms rather than a nominal family room. A couple may prefer a quieter property even if it is not the most famous address.

Use the [hotel planning guide](/journal/how-to-choose-hotels-china-itinerary) to compare location, room basis, breakfast, service level and confirmation status. A decision-ready proposal should say whether a hotel is confirmed, requested or illustrative, and what standard applies if a substitution is needed.

## 5. Define the service chain

Private guiding, driving, tickets, trains and operations support are different responsibilities. A proposal should show where the guide meets the traveler, who operates the vehicle, how station entry works, where luggage moves and who answers when the guide’s day has ended.

The [private guide versus driver explanation](/journal/private-guide-vs-driver-china) is useful when comparing quotations. A low headline price can mean that an important transfer, guide day or ticket is outside the scope. A high price is not automatically proof of quality either. The value is in clear responsibility and a route that works.

## 6. Read the proposal for certainty and limits

Separate confirmed services from planning assumptions. Hotels, rail tickets and timed attractions may depend on travel dates, passport details or official sales windows. A responsible proposal says what has been confirmed, what will be purchased later and what happens if the preferred option is unavailable.

Check the [private tour proposal checklist](/journal/private-china-tour-proposal-checklist) for the legal operator, price unit, room basis, inclusions, payment schedule and cancellation terms. These details are part of the product, not a footnote.

## Questions travelers ask

### How far in advance should I plan a private China trip?

Start early enough to compare gateways, hotels and route length before high-demand dates. The correct timing depends on season, party size and the number of reservation-dependent experiences. Ask the operator which items are date-sensitive rather than relying on one universal deadline.

### Can I change the itinerary after receiving a proposal?

Usually, yes, before services are confirmed. Changes can affect hotel availability, rail timing, guide coverage and price. Ask for the effect of each change rather than accepting a new total without an updated scope.

### Do I need a guide every day?

No. Guiding creates the most value where interpretation, complex movement or local communication matters. A free day can be independent when the hotel, transport and contact plan support it.

### What should I send a China travel specialist first?

Send dates, travelers, gateways, priorities, pace and any practical requirements. You do not need to know the perfect route before starting the conversation.
