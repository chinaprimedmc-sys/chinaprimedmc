# China from Singapore, Planned Privately

> [!summary] Editorial brief
> **Audience:** Singapore-based couples, families and small groups considering a private China holiday

## Draft

A well-planned private China tour from Singapore usually needs **8 to 12 days**, two to four carefully connected cities and one accountable team coordinating hotels, guides, transport, tickets and local support. For a first trip, Beijing, Xi’an and Shanghai form the clearest classic route. Families and food-focused travelers can extend to Chengdu, while repeat visitors may prefer Yunnan, Zhangjiajie, Sichuan or the Silk Road.

Singapore travelers have strong regional flight access and are often comfortable navigating Asian cities independently. The value of a private tour is therefore not simply having someone present every hour. It is having the difficult parts—route design, Chinese-language reservations, local transport, guide quality, attraction capacity and changes during the trip—connected properly.

## The 60-second route choice

- **First trip, 8–10 days:** Beijing, Xi’an and Shanghai.
- **Family trip, 11–12 days:** add Chengdu for pandas and food.
- **One-week break:** choose Beijing, Shanghai or Chengdu as the main base.
- **Repeat trip:** choose one region such as Yunnan, Sichuan or the Silk Road.

If flights are not booked, [ask AVIORA which city should come first](/start-planning?source=journal-singapore-route-choice). The right open-jaw ticket can remove a return flight and protect a day of the holiday.

## Start with the right trip length

### 6 to 7 days: choose one region

Use a short trip for Beijing and the Great Wall, Shanghai with a nearby extension, Chengdu and pandas, or Xi’an combined with Beijing. Avoid spending half the holiday changing hotels.

### 8 to 10 days: the classic first route

Beijing, Xi’an and Shanghai work well when history, the Great Wall and modern China all matter. High-speed rail connects Beijing and Xi’an; the onward sector to Shanghai can be chosen by total travel time and available schedule.

See the [10-day first-China itinerary](/journal/10-day-china-itinerary-first-time-visitors) for a realistic day-by-day framework.

### 11 to 12 days: add Chengdu

Add Chengdu for giant pandas, Sichuan food, tea-house culture and a softer contrast to the three major historical and commercial cities. The extra city needs more than one rushed night.

AVIORA’s [11-day Beijing, Xi’an, Chengdu and Shanghai private tour](/tours/beijing-xian-chengdu-shanghai-private-11-day-tour) uses rail and domestic air selectively to protect sightseeing time.

### 13 days or more: travel beyond the icons

Longer trips can include Yunnan, Guilin, Zhangjiajie, Jiuzhaigou or the Silk Road. The best addition depends on season, flight access, altitude, road time and the party’s tolerance for hotel changes.

## The best first-China route from Singapore

For most first-time visitors, begin in Beijing and finish in Shanghai.

Beijing provides the Forbidden City, Temple of Heaven, hutong context and a dedicated Great Wall day. Xi’an adds the Terracotta Army and an important historical transition. Shanghai creates a modern, internationally connected final chapter and often works well for the departure flight.

This open-jaw structure avoids returning to the first city only to fly home. Exact international services change by date, so compare a Singapore–Beijing arrival and Shanghai–Singapore departure with the reverse direction before confirming hotels.

Do not select the international ticket only by the lowest fare. Arrival time affects hotel nights and the first day; a very early departure can remove most of the final day. Airport choice, baggage allowance, connection conditions and whether all sectors are on one ticket also matter.

## Entry rules for Singapore passport holders

Singapore and China introduced a mutual visa-exemption arrangement in 2024 for eligible ordinary-passport holders making short visits. Entry conditions can change, and visa-free access does not remove the need to meet passport, purpose-of-visit, onward-travel and immigration requirements.

Before travel, check the current position with the Embassy of the People’s Republic of China in Singapore and Singapore’s Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Travelers using another passport, holding a temporary document, working, studying or staying beyond the permitted period may need a visa.

Keep accommodation and onward-travel details accessible. If the itinerary uses a transit policy instead of ordinary visa-free entry, the route and permitted area must be checked separately; read the [China 240-hour transit guide](/journal/china-240-hour-visa-free-transit-guide).

## Flights and arrival-city logic

China is not one airport market. Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Shenzhen, Chengdu, Chongqing, Kunming, Xi’an and other gateways can each be useful depending on the route and current airline schedule.

For a classic northern and eastern itinerary, an open-jaw international ticket is often the cleanest design. For Yunnan or Sichuan, a connection through a southern or western gateway may be more practical. For a short regional break, choose the destination first and the cheapest sensible flight second.

On arrival, private airport transfer has particular value for families, late flights, older parents and travelers carrying several large suitcases. The driver must be matched to the real luggage load; passenger count alone does not determine vehicle size.

## Hotels: compare location and room setup

Singapore travelers may recognize global hotel brands, but brand familiarity should not replace route logic. A hotel that saves thirty minutes of transfer twice a day can improve the trip more than a larger room far from the planned sights.

For families, confirm bedding and occupancy rules before payment. “Family room,” connecting rooms and an extra bed are not interchangeable. For older parents, ask about lift access, bathroom layout, breakfast distance and how far the vehicle can approach the entrance.

For a premium trip, request the room category in writing. An entry-level room at a luxury hotel should not be compared with a larger upgraded room under the same hotel name.

## Payments and mobile connectivity

Prepare payments before departure. International visitors can connect eligible overseas cards to supported Chinese mobile-payment services, but a resilient plan still includes more than one payment method.

Use the [China mobile payments guide](/journal/china-mobile-payments-foreign-tourists) to prepare Alipay or Weixin Pay, card backups and RMB cash. Do not wait until an airport transfer or first restaurant bill to discover that a bank verification step is incomplete.

Mobile data supports maps, translation, booking messages and payment authentication. Compare Singapore roaming, an eligible eSIM and a local physical SIM against device compatibility and access requirements. The [China SIM and eSIM guide](/journal/china-sim-card-esim-internet-foreign-tourists) explains the practical differences.

## Family and multi-generational planning

The route should account for Singapore school-holiday dates, China public holidays and the physical load of major attractions. Summer heat, winter conditions and holiday crowds affect children and grandparents differently.

For a family trip:

- alternate long heritage days with lighter food, neighborhood or transfer periods;
- book a vehicle around passengers and luggage;
- confirm room occupancy and connecting arrangements;
- use guides who can adjust explanations for children and adults;
- protect meals and rest rather than treating them as gaps; and
- avoid adding another city simply because the map makes it look close.

Pandas can be an excellent family focus, but Chengdu deserves enough time for more than one morning attraction. See AVIORA’s [Chengdu pandas and Sichuan table journey](/tours/chengdu-pandas-sichuan-table).

## Muslim-friendly private travel in China

A Muslim-friendly China itinerary must be planned city by city. Halal restaurant availability, prayer timing, guide knowledge, hotel location and the exact day route all matter. A generic label is not enough.

In Xi’an, the Muslim Quarter and Great Mosque area provide important historical context, but the itinerary still needs current restaurant checks. In other cities, a suitable meal may require a vehicle transfer or a different sightseeing sequence. Communicate whether the requirement is halal-certified food, no pork, seafood or vegetarian alternatives, prayer access or a combination.

AVIORA treats these as planning requirements to be reconfirmed with the guide and suppliers. No responsible operator should promise that every venue or hotel kitchen meets a specific standard without current written confirmation.

## Private tour, group package or independent trip?

Choose a group package if the fixed departure, price and shared schedule are more important than flexibility. Travel independently if the party is comfortable handling Chinese-language bookings, transport changes and attraction reservations. Choose a private tour when the route, pace, hotel logic and local support need to fit specific people.

A private plan is particularly useful for:

- families traveling during school holidays;
- couples who want better hotels and unhurried days;
- older parents or mobility-aware travel;
- Muslim-friendly or dietary planning;
- photographers and food-focused travelers; and
- small groups that want one vehicle and guide team.

## What a Singapore quotation should state

Ask for a written proposal showing:

1. dates, route and number of nights in each city;
2. hotel names, room categories, breakfast and taxes;
3. guide language and daily service coverage;
4. vehicle type and luggage assumption;
5. train or domestic-flight class;
6. included attraction tickets and meals;
7. arrival and departure transfers;
8. special dietary, family or mobility arrangements;
9. exclusions, deposit and cancellation conditions; and
10. who provides assistance while you are in China.

## When to book

Begin earlier for Singapore school holidays, China public holidays, premium family rooms and routes using capacity-controlled attractions. The objective is not to lock every meal months ahead; it is to secure the elements with limited availability and keep flexible parts flexible.

If dates are not final, provide a likely week and acceptable alternatives. A useful operator can identify where shifting by one or two days changes the route materially.

## A practical pre-departure checklist

### About 30 days before travel

Confirm passport validity and current entry rules, international flight airports, hotel room setup, route order and any dietary or mobility requirements. Complete the decisions that affect supplier availability.

### About 7 days before travel

Check the final train or domestic-flight details, attraction reservations, guide contacts, airport meeting instructions, weather and the payment apps or cards you intend to use.

### Before leaving Singapore

Keep passports, confirmations, hotel addresses and the local support number accessible offline. Verify the first airport terminal and do not pack essential medicine or every payment method in checked baggage.

## FAQ

### Do Singapore citizens need a visa for China?

Eligible Singapore ordinary-passport holders currently benefit from a mutual short-stay visa-exemption arrangement, subject to the current rules and immigration decision. Check official sources before departure and confirm separately for other passports or travel purposes.

### How many days should a first China trip from Singapore be?

Eight to twelve days works well for most first trips. Ten days suits Beijing, Xi’an and Shanghai; eleven or twelve days allows Chengdu to be added more comfortably.

### What is the best China itinerary for families from Singapore?

Beijing, Xi’an and Shanghai provide a strong first route. Add Chengdu when pandas and food are priorities, but reduce the number of other cities or extend the trip.

### Can a private China tour accommodate halal food and prayer time?

Yes, as a planning requirement, but restaurants, menus, locations and timing must be checked city by city. State the exact requirements before the proposal is prepared.

### Should I book international flights before requesting a private proposal?

Not necessarily. Request the route first if you are unsure which city should be the arrival or departure gateway. If flights are already booked, provide the exact airports, dates and times.
