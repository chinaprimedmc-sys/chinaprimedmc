## Draft

# The Zero-Shopping Guarantee: Why True Luxury Travel in China Means Absolute Operational Transparency

For a private family trip, the useful question is not whether shopping exists in China. It is whether a shop visit is required to receive the sightseeing and guiding described in the itinerary.

AVIORA's published private-tour standard excludes compulsory shopping stops. Families can still request a market, craft studio, or shop when it fits their interests. The distinction is whether the stop is an informed choice or a condition of the tour.

## What a clear no-forced-shopping policy means

A policy is practical when the written itinerary makes the day's purpose legible. Ask the operator to identify the planned attractions, transfers, meals, optional activities, and any time reserved for independent browsing. If shopping is not a priority, request that preference in writing and ask how it will be reflected in the final route.

| Planning question                  | What to confirm in writing                                                                            |
| ---------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Is a retail visit required?        | Confirm that sightseeing and guide service do not depend on entering a store or making a purchase.    |
| Can we visit a market or workshop? | Identify the specific activity, its purpose, and whether participation or purchases are optional.     |
| What does the price cover?         | List hotels and room basis, transport, guide days, admissions, meals, and any supplements separately. |
| What can change?                   | Note supplier-dependent details, seasonal adjustments, and how replacements are approved.             |

## How AVIORA describes its policy

AVIORA states that its private journeys do not include forced shopping. On the published 12-day family route, no compulsory factory, showroom, or retail stop is inserted into sightseeing. The itinerary may include a market visit when it is a stated cultural or culinary activity; purchases remain optional.

This is a service policy, not a claim that every cultural activity is free of commerce or that every supplier-controlled detail can be guaranteed before confirmation. If a family wants a specific shopping-free condition included in its booking terms, it should ask for the exact wording to appear in the proposal and final agreement.

### Distinguish a cultural activity from a sales stop

A market or workshop can be meaningful when it has a clear place in the route. For example, a market visit may support a planned cooking activity, while a craft demonstration may explain a local technique. The itinerary should say what the family will do, how long the activity is expected to take, and whether a purchase is optional. A visit should not be described only with a vague label such as “local experience” if the family cannot tell whether it is a retail stop.

Families can also state preferences directly: no retail stops, no shopping time, or a specific market visit with no expectation to buy. These are different requests. The operator can then reflect the chosen scope in the daily plan and confirm any admission, workshop, or transport cost before booking.

### Review the day as a sequence

Look at the full day rather than counting attraction names. The proposal should make transfers and approximate visit windows understandable, especially when travelers include children or older adults. Ask where the vehicle meets the group, whether the guide stays with the family during each visit, and what happens if a planned activity runs late or closes. This helps distinguish a real schedule from a list of places with no indication of how the day will work.

For a multigenerational party, check whether the itinerary has room for seated breaks, meal timing, and a return to the hotel when needed. A private tour allows more flexibility than a fixed group departure, but opening hours, traffic, tickets and supplier availability still affect the day. The written plan should identify which parts are fixed and which can be adjusted with the guide.

## Check the price basis, not just the headline

The 12-day family journey publishes a starting estimate of **US$5,850 per person** for two adults and two children aged 6-11 sharing two hotel-confirmed rooms during non-peak periods. It is a defined example, not a universal rate for every family. Dates, ages, room configuration, peak periods, and requested additions affect the final quotation.

Before booking, compare proposals using the same scope. Confirm the hotel room setup, private vehicle and guide service, train or flight class, admissions, included meals, optional activities, and any items paid locally. A clear proposal makes it possible to understand both the service and its limits.

## A practical review checklist

- Read each day's stops and identify which activities are included, optional, or available only on request.
- Ask whether a market, workshop, or retail venue is required, and whether any purchase is expected.
- Confirm the specific room configuration with the hotels before treating connecting rooms or a suite as secured.
- Check the transport, guide, admission, meal, and luggage arrangements against your family's needs.
- Keep the final itinerary and all agreed exclusions or preferences in writing.

## Common questions

### Does zero shopping mean the tour never visits a market?

No. It means a purchase or retail stop is not compulsory. A market can be included when the family chooses it for food, local life, or another stated interest. Confirm whether the activity is optional and whether any purchases are at the family's discretion.

### Can the no-forced-shopping policy be written into our booking terms?

Ask for the exact policy language in the proposal and final agreement. AVIORA's published private-tour standard excludes compulsory shopping, and the booking documents should make the agreed itinerary and service scope clear for your trip.

### Is US$5,850 the price for every family of four?

No. It is the published starting estimate for two adults and two children aged 6-11 sharing two hotel-confirmed rooms outside peak periods. Different dates, ages, room requirements and optional services can change the quote. Request a date-specific proposal before treating the starting estimate as your trip total.

### What should we compare between proposals?

Compare the same dates and passenger details, then check hotel names and room basis, private transport, guide coverage, train or flight class, admissions, meals, optional activities and exclusions. Ask who pays any costs not included and whether proposed room configurations have been confirmed by the hotel.
