# Chengdu, Pandas & Sichuan Table — 5 nights / 6 days

**One-line summary:** A slower western-China interlude built around giant pandas, teahouse afternoons, and the layered heat and fragrance of Sichuan cooking.

## Research summary (Step 1)

**Why this is a proven Western leisure route:** Chengdu appears repeatedly as a 2–3 night stop in Wendy Wu Tours, China Highlights, WildChina, G Adventures and TourRadar programs, usually paired with Xi’an, Chongqing or a wider China circuit. Panda-base visits and food experiences are the consistent anchors; operator reviews frequently praise private guides who can make the food accessible.

**Common duration:** 4–6 nights (often 2–3 Chengdu nights plus Leshan or Chongqing).

**Typical sequence:**

1. Arrive Chengdu; relaxed Jinli or Kuixinglou evening.
2. Early Chengdu Research Base; lunch and People’s Park teahouse.
3. Sichuan cooking class or food walk; Wuhou Shrine and old neighbourhoods.
4. Leshan Giant Buddha day trip, or a free Chengdu day in shorter versions.
5. Transfer onward to Xi’an/Chongqing, commonly by high-speed rail.

**Usually included:** High-speed rail or private vehicle between cities, private or small-group English-speaking guide, entrance tickets, breakfast and selected lunches/dinners. The mainstream version tends to fix restaurant menus and run a full sightseeing day around the panda opening hours.

**Typical audience:** First-time China visitors, families with children, food-minded couples, and repeat visitors looking beyond the imperial cities.

**Working source pattern:** Wendy Wu and G Adventures catalogue Chengdu as a repeatable western-China highlight; China Highlights/WildChina use panda-base + food culture as the core private-day formula; TourRadar listings show 5–8 day Chengdu/Xi’an combinations and review emphasis on guides and logistics.

## Comparative analysis (Step 2)

**Typical version:** A fixed early departure to the panda base, then a tightly packed city loop; guide quality is signalled by “English-speaking” or “licensed,” with little detail on vetting. Group departures and set restaurants limit pace. Stairs, heat and long standing are rarely explained for older travellers.

**Our version:** We reserve the first panda entry and keep the rest of the day deliberately light: the guide watches energy levels, finds shade and builds in a proper teahouse pause. A privately vetted food guide can order around spice tolerance, allergies and children rather than delivering a preset banquet. The Leshan day is optional; a private car can stop early, return to Chengdu, or replace it with a market and cooking afternoon. WhatsApp access means a parent can change the next morning without negotiating a call centre.

## Client-facing copy (Step 3)

### The route

Chengdu is a city with a soft centre. The pandas are the obvious beginning, but the deeper pleasure is the rhythm around them: tea poured slowly in a park, peppercorn and citrus on a shared table, a neighbourhood that still feels lived in after the tour buses leave. This private journey gives Chengdu enough time to become more than a photo stop.

### Day by day

**Day 1 — Arrive into Chengdu’s easy pace**  
Your private driver meets you at the airport or station and settles you into the city without adding a sightseeing obligation. In the evening, your guide can suggest a gentle walk through Kuixinglou or a first Sichuan dinner chosen for your group’s comfort with spice.

**Day 2 — Pandas before the day begins**  
We enter the Chengdu Research Base early, when the pandas are most active and the paths are quieter. Your guide keeps the route short and shaded where possible, then the private car returns you to the city for lunch and an unhurried cup of tea in People’s Park. Children can run; grandparents can simply watch the city move.

**Day 3 — A city understood through taste**  
The morning might take in Wuhou Shrine and the old lanes around it. Later, a personally vetted local food guide leads a market visit or a small cooking lesson, adjusting the menu to allergies, appetite and heat tolerance. Nothing is compulsory; a second tea house or an afternoon nap is a perfectly good plan.

**Day 4 — Leshan, or more Chengdu**  
For those who want the scale of the Leshan Giant Buddha, we drive privately and choose the least strenuous viewing option. If the river cliffs feel like too much, we stay in Chengdu for a slower day of galleries, a neighbourhood market and a long lunch. The route flexes around your people, not a bus timetable.

**Day 5 — A final morning, then onward**  
There is time for a final bowl of noodles, a quiet park walk or a lie-in before your driver takes you to the station. We remain reachable as you continue to Xi’an, Chongqing or your next China chapter.

### Who it suits

Families who want a genuine child-sized highlight without exhausting the adults; older travellers who prefer comfort and choice over a checklist; and repeat China visitors who want food culture, not another sequence of monumental entrances.

### Practical notes

The panda base involves walking on mostly manageable paths, but early starts and occasional slopes matter. Spring and autumn are comfortable; summer is warmer and busier. Sichuan food can be tailored from fragrant and mild to properly fiery—tell us your boundaries before you arrive.
