# Shanghai, Suzhou & the Yangtze — 8 nights / 9 days

**One-line summary:** A measured passage from Jiangnan gardens to the Three Gorges, balancing elegant water towns with the ease of unpacking once for the river.

## Research summary (Step 1)

**Why this is a proven Western leisure route:** Viking, AmaWaterways, Victoria Cruises, Sanctuary Retreats, Wendy Wu and China Highlights repeatedly package Yangtze cruises with Shanghai, Suzhou, Chongqing and/or Xi’an. Western reviews consistently value the low-friction ship days and Three Gorges scenery; shore excursions are commonly the pressure point.

**Common duration:** 7–10 nights including a 3–4 night cruise.

**Typical sequence:**

1. Shanghai arrival.
2. Suzhou day trip or overnight; classical garden and canal.
3. Transfer to Chongqing; embark.
   4–6. Cruise through Fengdu/Wushan, White Emperor City and Three Gorges; shore excursions.
4. Yichang disembark; Three Gorges Dam.
5. Transfer onward or return to Shanghai.

**Usually included:** Cabin, meals onboard, shore excursions, coach transfers and group English-speaking cruise guides. Land extensions may be private, but cruise excursions are often fixed and early.

**Typical audience:** Older travellers, multi-generation groups, first-time visitors wanting comfort, and travellers who dislike changing hotels nightly.

**Working source pattern:** Viking/Ama/China Navigation market the cruise as a Western-friendly cultural journey; Victoria and China Highlights show the recurring Shanghai–Chongqing–Yichang frame; review patterns focus on cabin comfort, meals, shore crowds and guide organisation.

## Comparative analysis (Step 2)

**Typical version:** The ship provides comfort, but shore days follow group coaches, fixed departure bells and a one-size-fits-all walking pace. Guide quality varies by excursion; meals are mostly predetermined. Suzhou is frequently reduced to a rushed day trip.

**Our version:** We use the cruise for what it does best—restful movement—and make the land before and after it private. Suzhou gets an overnight when the group wants garden quiet. On shore, our guide can skip a crowded optional excursion, arrange a shorter route or stay with a family member who needs a slower pace; private car transfers handle the edges of the cruise itinerary.

## Client-facing copy (Step 3)

### The route

The Yangtze changes the way a China journey feels. Instead of packing and unpacking through the interior, you wake to a new stretch of river. This version pairs that ease with the cultivated calm of Suzhou and Shanghai, leaving enough private time on land for the people who make the journey.

### Day by day

**Day 1 — Shanghai, arrival gently**  
Your driver meets you and takes you to a well-placed hotel. Depending on arrival, we keep the first evening to the Bund lights, a short neighbourhood walk or room service and sleep.

**Day 2 — Suzhou’s garden grammar**  
A private car carries you to Suzhou. Your guide chooses one exceptional garden and a quieter canal lane rather than rushing through a catalogue. Stay overnight if you want the city after the day-trippers leave; return to Shanghai if that suits your flight or energy better.

**Day 3 — Shanghai to Chongqing**  
We arrange the most comfortable connection and meet you in Chongqing. A light introduction to the hill-city’s river views leads to embarkation, with your luggage settled for the next few nights.

**Days 4–6 — The river does the travelling**  
Life onboard is intentionally unhurried: breakfast with a changing view, a lecture or none, and excursions chosen with your guide. We can take the shorter walking option at Fengdu or Wushan, linger on deck through the Qutang Gorge and return to the ship before the group rushes.

**Day 7 — Dam and disembarkation**  
At Yichang, we visit the Three Gorges Dam if it is meaningful to you, then transfer privately onward. Those who prefer can go straight to the station or airport.

**Days 8–9 — Shanghai, by choice**  
Finish with two nights in Shanghai for the French Concession, a museum, a market or a free day. We keep the final rhythm open so the trip ends rested rather than over-programmed.

### Who it suits

Older travellers and multi-generation families who value a lift, a proper cabin and fewer hotel changes; first-timers who want a gentle structure; and anyone who would rather watch a landscape unfold than chase it.

### Practical notes

Cruise schedules and shore calls vary by sailing date. Decks can be windy and shore ramps uneven. Spring and autumn are comfortable; winter sailings are quieter but cooler. We confirm the exact ship, cabin location and excursion mobility before booking.
