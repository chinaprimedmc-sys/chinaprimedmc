# China with Pandas — Beijing, Xi’an, Chengdu & Shanghai

**One-line summary:** A 12-day, 11-night first-China journey for families and multi-generation groups, adding Chengdu’s pandas, food and softer pace to the classic city arc.

## Research summary (Step 1)

### 市场地位与证据

成都长期被Wendy Wu、G Adventures、Intrepid、China Highlights、WildChina和TourRadar加入黄金三角，形成10–14天家庭版或“China highlights with pandas”。它不是偶然附加点：熊猫拥有跨年龄、跨文化的直接吸引力，成都—西安之间的高铁又使组合具备稳定运营基础。

### 常见时长与顺序

- 常见时长：10–14天；舒适版通常12天左右。
- 主流顺序：北京3–4晚 → 西安2晚 → 成都2–3晚 → 上海2–3晚。
- 核心景点：故宫、长城、兵马俑、熊猫基地、人民公园/川菜、上海外滩与街区。

### 市场通常包含

高铁与国内航班、城市内巴士或私人车、英文导游、熊猫基地早场、部分团餐。普通家庭团会用“family-friendly”营销，但依然遵循统一集合时间；儿童饮食、午睡和祖辈体力很少真正改变线路。

### 常见营销对象

有6–16岁孩子的西方家庭、多代家庭、希望用一个明确儿童亮点平衡历史景点的首次访华者。

## Comparative analysis (Step 2)

### Typical version

为了控制总天数，成都往往只有两晚：抵达、第二天熊猫基地加多个市区景点、第三天离开。熊猫早起之后仍继续全天活动；固定川菜菜单忽略儿童、过敏和辣度；北京与西安段仍保持成人团体节奏。

### Our version

- 保留北京4天、西安2天、成都3天、上海3天，交通日不伪装成完整观光日。
- 熊猫基地是当天主体验，下午只有茶馆和可选择的轻活动。
- 一位家庭成员想回酒店时，私人车可以分段支持，而不是全家被迫继续。
- 川菜体验按辣度、过敏、儿童熟悉度选择；不会用“正宗”要求客户忍受不适。
- 家庭在规划期和落地期都能直接联系真人，临时改动不经过呼叫中心。

## Client-facing copy (Step 3)

### Overview

The best family journeys give every generation something to claim as their own. Beijing has scale, Xi’an has a story children can see, Chengdu has pandas and a table worth sharing, and Shanghai brings the trip to an easy modern finish. Private transport keeps the complicated parts quiet in the background.

### Day by day

**Day 1 — Arrive in Beijing**  
Meet your driver and settle in. The first evening is left deliberately empty.

**Day 2 — The Forbidden City, made readable**  
Follow a carefully chosen palace route with breaks, questions and room for children to notice details. Finish early or add a short park visit.

**Day 3 — Beijing beyond the palace walls**  
Begin at the Temple of Heaven, then choose a hutong activity, the Summer Palace or a free afternoon. The guide adjusts explanation and movement to the ages traveling.

**Day 4 — A family Great Wall day**  
Travel by private car to the Wall section best suited to the season and your group. Cable-car options, shorter walks and an earlier return are all valid choices.

**Day 5 — High-speed rail to Xi’an**  
We coordinate tickets, stations and luggage. A city-wall view or relaxed dinner is enough after arrival.

**Day 6 — Warriors, archaeology and Xi’an food**  
The Terracotta Army gives history a human face. Your guide can keep the story clear for children while answering deeper questions from adults. Food or rest fills the afternoon by choice.

**Day 7 — Rail to Chengdu**  
Continue southwest by high-speed rail. Your driver meets the family and takes you to a calm hotel base. Dinner is selected around appetite and spice confidence.

**Day 8 — Pandas before the crowds**  
Enter early, when feeding and movement are most likely. We use a manageable route and leave when the family is ready. The afternoon slows down over tea in People’s Park.

**Day 9 — Chengdu through food and daily life**  
Choose a market, cooking experience or Wuhou Shrine and neighbourhood walk. Children can participate; grandparents can sit out a section without losing the day.

**Day 10 — Fly to Shanghai**  
Private transfers make the airport day simple. If energy remains, see the riverfront lights; if not, Shanghai can wait until morning.

**Day 11 — A modern city at family pace**  
Combine architecture, local streets, food and one child-friendly interest. Avoid turning the final day into compulsory shopping.

**Day 12 — Departure**  
Keep breakfast unhurried before your private airport transfer.

### Who this route suits

Families with school-age children, grandparents traveling with grandchildren, and first-time visitors who need a clear child-sized highlight without sacrificing adult cultural depth.

### Practical notes

The panda base requires an early start and several kilometres of possible walking. Beijing and the Terracotta Army also involve large sites. We plan rest days and vehicle support, but families should still bring comfortable shoes. Summer heat and Chinese school holidays affect crowd levels.
