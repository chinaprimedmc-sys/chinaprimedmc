# First China, Beautifully Paced — Beijing, Xi’an & Shanghai

**One-line summary:** China’s essential first chapter in 9 days and 8 nights, with imperial Beijing, historic Xi’an and modern Shanghai connected by private care rather than a group timetable.

## Research summary (Step 1)

### 市场地位与证据

北京—西安—上海通常被称为China Golden Triangle，是Western-facing China产品中最稳定的首次访华结构。Wendy Wu、Intrepid、G Adventures、Audley、China Highlights、WildChina、Viking陆地段及TourRadar的不同价位产品长期重复这一城市顺序。差异主要是8–12天、团体或私人、是否加入长城段落与上海自由日，而不是是否采用这三个城市。

### 常见时长

- 紧凑团体版：8天7晚
- 主流版本：9–10天
- 舒适私人版：11–12天
- 本版本：9天8晚，北京4天、西安2天、上海3天

### 常见城市与景点顺序

1. 北京抵达。
2. 天安门与故宫。
3. 天坛、胡同或颐和园。
4. 长城。
5. 高铁前往西安，城墙或回民街。
6. 兵马俑。
7. 飞往上海，外滩。
8. 老城、法租界或现代城市体验。
9. 离境。

### 市场通常包含

- 团体巴士或私人车辆；北京—西安通常为高铁，西安—上海通常为航班。
- Licensed/English-speaking guide，但很少解释导游如何筛选。
- 早餐及部分团餐，购物站在低价产品中仍常见。
- 固定早出发、固定餐厅、固定集合时间。
- 家庭与长者通常只得到“可参加”标签，真实步行量解释不足。

### 常见营销对象

第一次访华者、退休夫妇、家庭、文化兴趣旅客，以及希望在一次旅行中理解中国主要历史阶段的客户。

## Comparative analysis (Step 2)

### Typical version

常见版本每天安排两个以上大型景点。故宫和长城可能连续两天高强度行走；西安为了配合交通只停一晚；上海容易被压缩成外滩、豫园和购物。团体共同行动意味着孩子疲倦或长者想提前回酒店时，整个团队无法改变。

### Our version

- 北京保留4天，让故宫、天坛和长城各自成为主体验，而不是同一天的打卡项目。
- 长城段落根据季节、体力和缆车条件选择，不承诺一个固定段落适合所有人。
- 西安兵马俑日不再叠加多个夜间演出；晚上是否继续由当天状态决定。
- 上海保留3天，把最后一站作为恢复和理解现代中国的章节，不安排强制购物。
- 每一城市都有私人车、私人司机和经人工筛选的英文导游；计划阶段和落地阶段都能直接联系真人。
- 行程可缩短、跳过或延长，不需要等团体投票。

## Client-facing copy (Step 3)

### Overview

For a first journey, China can feel impossibly large. This route gives it a clear shape. Beijing holds the scale of empire; Xi’an brings the distant past into human detail; Shanghai shows how quickly the country continues to change. Nine private days are enough to understand the arc, provided each day has room to breathe.

### Day by day

**Day 1 — Arrive in Beijing**  
Your private driver meets you at the airport and takes you directly to your hotel. There is no arrival-day sightseeing obligation. If the flight lands early and everyone feels well, your guide can suggest a short neighbourhood walk; otherwise the evening belongs to rest.

**Day 2 — The Forbidden City, with context**  
Enter the palace complex with a guide who knows how to turn scale into a readable story. We choose a sensible route through the courtyards, pause for photographs and sit when the group needs to. The afternoon remains flexible: a quiet park, a hutong introduction or an early return.

**Day 3 — Temple life and everyday Beijing**  
Visit the Temple of Heaven while the surrounding park is alive with local routines. Later, choose between a shorter hutong walk, a hands-on family activity or the Summer Palace. We do not treat every option as compulsory.

**Day 4 — The Great Wall at your pace**  
Your private car carries you beyond the city. The final Wall section and walking route are chosen around weather, crowd levels, cable-car access and the knees in your group. The evening is left open; the Wall should be the memory of the day.

**Day 5 — High-speed rail to Xi’an**  
We manage station timing, tickets and luggage flow, then meet you in Xi’an. After check-in, take a gentle look at the city wall or keep the evening for dinner and sleep.

**Day 6 — The Terracotta Army**  
Spend the morning with the excavation pits and the individual faces of the warriors. Your guide adjusts the depth of explanation to the group, from an accessible family story to detailed archaeology. The afternoon can hold Xi’an food culture or a restorative hotel break.

**Day 7 — Xi’an to Shanghai**  
Fly east with private transfers at both ends. Shanghai’s first impression can be as simple as a walk beside the Huangpu River as the lights come on.

**Day 8 — Old and new Shanghai**  
Move between riverfront history, tree-lined neighbourhoods, local food and contemporary architecture. The day is shaped around your interests; shopping is never inserted unless you ask for it.

**Day 9 — A measured farewell**  
Use the final morning for breakfast, a short walk or a lie-in. Your driver takes you to the airport with a practical margin for your flight.

### Who this route suits

- First-time visitors who want a coherent introduction rather than maximum city count.
- Families whose children need variety and recovery time.
- Older travellers who want private transport and honest walking expectations.
- Couples and multi-generation groups who value direct human support.

### Practical notes

Spring and autumn are generally comfortable. Beijing and Xi’an involve large sites and unavoidable walking; private vehicles reduce transfers but cannot remove every palace courtyard or museum distance. Forbidden City and rail tickets need advance booking. Summer is hot and school-holiday periods are busy.
